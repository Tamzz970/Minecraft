A big fish shot toward
the little octopus.

No time to lose!

The octopus squirted water
from its body
and zoomed away,
swimming as hard as it could.

But inside its body,
something strange
was happening.

One of its three hearts
stopped beating.

It was the big heart,
the one that sends blood
through the whole body.

Every time an octopus swims,
that heart goes quiet.

The other two hearts
kept beating,
pushing blood through the gills
to take oxygen from the water.

Its blood is blue,
because it contains copper,
a metal used to make wires.

That blue blood carries oxygen,
but without the big heart,
its body gets tired fast.

The octopus slipped
into a crack in the rocks.

The big fish circled around,
then swam away.

Slowly, the heart
that had stopped
began to beat again.

This time, the octopus
did not swim.

It crawled along
the sea floor,
and all three of its hearts
beat together.
